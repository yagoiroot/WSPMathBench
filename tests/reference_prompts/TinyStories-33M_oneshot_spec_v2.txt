TASK. You are given a description of the weights of a language model, TinyStories-33M (4 layers of width 768 with 16 heads of size 48; layers 0 and 2 attend to the whole context, layers 1 and 3 to a window of 256 tokens; each layer has a GELU MLP of width 3,072; LayerNorm with bias; learned position embeddings for 2,048 positions; vocabulary 50,257; input embedding and output layer tied). Your job is to make it better at basic arithmetic by specifying an edit to its weights, written in the edit language below: a list of operations that are applied together. You cannot run the model or any code. You reply once.

HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED. After you finish, your edited model and the unedited model are evaluated on BasicArithmetic (500 very easy questions: 250 additions, subtractions, multiplications and divisions, mostly of single-digit numbers, each written once with digits and symbols and once in words; the symbolic questions come with and without spaces, "2 + 2 =" and "2+2=", and multiplication is written "x" or "*") with this fixed protocol: the model continues the question text exactly as written (no chat template, nothing added before or after it); temperature 0.1, at most 16 new tokens; the first number in the continuation, written in digits or in words, is graded against the reference answer. The config, tokenizer, prompt format and decoding settings are fixed: only weight values can change (same tensor names and shapes). You are not told which questions this benchmark holds.

WHAT COUNTS. The goal is a model that scores higher under that protocol, and how you get there is open. You might remove something that holds the model back, strengthen something it already does well, or change how some part of it computes. The edit may be narrow (a few neurons, one head) or broad (a direction or a pattern across many layers), and it may pursue more than one of these at once. No route is preferred. The practice results and examples further down describe how the model behaves now: they are evidence to reason from, and they do not mark out what your edit should target. What we ask in every case is that the edit comes from understanding the network: say which components you are changing (neurons, heads, directions, circuits or larger structures), what you believe they do, and why changing them should make the model more accurate on problems it has not seen. Out of scope, and refused: the final norm (transformer.ln_f.weight), the final norm's bias (transformer.ln_f.bias), the input embedding (transformer.wte.weight), the position embedding (transformer.wpe.weight) and the output layer (lm_head.weight), and any edit that only multiplies a whole tensor by a constant. An edit that only makes the whole output distribution sharper or flatter is not what we are looking for either: that is a decoding setting reached through the weights, and it shows nothing about the network. Nor is an edit that makes the model give the same answer, or one of a few fixed answers, whatever the problem says (for example by pushing the output toward a particular number): that can score above zero by matching the most common answers, but it removes the model’s dependence on the problem instead of improving how it solves one.
